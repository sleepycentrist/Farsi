## Lesson notes — 4 October 2026: jobs and what people do

### Vocabulary and clarifications
- **kudak / koodak** (کودک) = child, another word for **bache**. **Nevisande-ye ketâb-e kudak** = children's book writer; **nevisande-ye ketâbhâ-ye kudakân** = writer of children's books.
- **sakht** (سخت) = difficult / hard.
- **khosusi** (خصوصی) = private (not “ghoosoosi”).
- **tu / tu-ye** (تو / توی) = in / inside, spoken; formal **dar**.
- **bakhsh-e mâli** (بخش مالی) = finance / financial department.
- **adad** (عدد) = number; **a'dâd** (اعداد) = numbers. **Âdat** (عادت) means habit, so “adat = number” needs this spelling distinction.
- **Bâ ... sar-o-kâr dâram** (با ... سر و کار دارم) = I deal with ... . Infinitive: **sar-o-kâr dâshtan**; use **bâ** for what/who you deal with.
- **marizhâ** (مریض‌ها) = patients / sick people, like **bimârhâ** (بیمارها).
- **mardom** (مردم) = people; **moshtari** (مشتری) = customer.
- **vakil** (وکیل) = lawyer; **vekâlat** (وکالت) = legal representation / practising law. **Daftar-e vekâlat** (دفتر وکالت) is the usual term for a law office. **Daftar-e vakil** can also literally mean a lawyer's office; it is not forbidden.
- **dâd** (داد) = justice in this context; **dâdgâh** (دادگاه) = court.
- **defâ' mikonam** (دفاع می‌کنم) = I defend. **Defâ' kardan az ...** = to defend ...; use **az** for the person/thing defended.
- **mosâfer** (مسافر) = passenger / traveller; **mosâferhâ** = passengers / travellers.
- **darmân** (درمان) = treatment; **darmân mikonam** = I treat; **bimârâ-ro** (بیمارا رو) = the patients as the object (spoken **bimârhâ râ**). **Ro** marks the object, rather than simply meaning “the.”
- **shoghl** (شغل) = job / occupation.
- **dars midam** (درس می‌دم) = I teach / give lessons; formal **dars midaham**, infinitive **dars dâdan**. **Dars mikhunam** = I study; **khândan** = to read (infinitive), **mikhânad / mikhune** = he/she reads.
- **honarmand** (هنرمند) = artist; **naqqâsh / naghâsh** (نقاش) = painter (a kind of artist).
- **maze** (مزه) = taste / flavour; **khoshmaze** = tasty; **badmaze** = bad-tasting / unpleasant-tasting; **bimaze** = tasteless / bland. **Bad** alone = bad.
- **khosh** (خوش) = pleasant / happy / good, used in many compounds. It is still a current word, not merely an ancient replacement for **khub** (good).
- **dorost** = correct; **dorost kardan** = to make / prepare (also fix in context).
- **chub** (چوب) = wood; **ketâbkhâne** = library, or bookcase in this furniture context.
- **khâmush kardan** (خاموش کردن) = to turn off / extinguish; correction of “harmoosh kardan.”
- **âtash**, spoken **âtish** (آتش), = fire. **Atash** (عطش), with a short initial vowel and different spelling, = thirst. **Teshne** = thirsty; **teshnegi** = thirst. These are distinct words, not one word derived from the other.
- **dânesh** (دانش) = knowledge / learning; **dâneshâmuz** (دانش‌آموز) = school pupil / student; **dâneshju** (دانشجو) = university student. The components suggest “knowledge learner” and “knowledge seeker”; dâneshju does not normally mean science researcher.

### Job questions, corrected into spoken Farsi
With **ki** (who), use the third-person verb: **mide / mikone**, rather than **midam / mikonam** (I).

| Question | Meaning | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Ki tu madrese dars mide? | Who teaches at school? | Mo'allem — teacher |
| Ki tu dâneshgâh dars mide? | Who teaches at university? | Ostâd — professor / lecturer |
| Ki tu gâleri-ye honari kâr mikone? | Who works in an art gallery? | Honarmand / naqqâsh — artist / painter (lesson answer) |

## Lesson notes — 27 September 2026: cooking

Sentence: **Man ghazâhâ-ye khoshmaze mipazam.** — I cook tasty dishes.
Persian: من غذاهای خوشمزه می‌پزم.
User's original transliteration: “man gazahaya ghoshmaze mipazam”.

- **khoshmaze** (خوشمزه) = tasty / delicious. Spelling clarification: khoshmaze, rather than ghoshmaze.
- **mipazam** (می‌پزم) = I cook / I am cooking. Infinitive: **pokhtan** (پختن), to cook; present stem **paz**, past stem **pokht**.
- **ghazâhâ-ye** (غذاهای) = foods / dishes + the linking sound before the adjective.
- **dorost mikonam** (درست می‌کنم) = I make / prepare; with food, I make / prepare food: **ghazâ dorost mikonam**.
- **dorost** (درست) alone = correct / right. **Dorost kardan** (درست کردن) is the infinitive meaning to make / prepare (also to fix, depending on context); **dorost kardam** (درست کردم) = I made / prepared.
- Memory aid from the lesson: “doing correctly.” Treat this as a mnemonic, rather than the literal translation of the compound verb when it means making food.

### Hair and hairdressers — same lesson

- **kutâh / kootâh** (کوتاه) = short; **kutâh kardan** = to shorten / cut short; **kutâh mikonam** = I shorten / cut. With hair, this means I cut / trim.
- **ârâyeshgar** (آرایشگر) = hairdresser / hairstylist. **-gar** and **-ande** can form words for someone who performs an activity, including occupations (e.g. ârâyeshgar, rânande = driver). They are not interchangeable endings or always job labels.
- **boland / booland** (بلند) = long / tall. **Man muhâ-ye bolandi dâram** (من موهای بلندی دارم) = I have long hair.
- **Boland kardan** with hair = to grow / let it grow long. **Man muhâmo boland kardam** (من موهامو بلند کردم) = I grew my hair long. **To muhâto boland kardi** = You grew your hair long.
- Original note: “man moohasho booland kardeh” — intended as making hair longer. Clarification: **muhâsho** means his/her hair + object marker, not my/your hair; **karde** alone does not agree with **man**. **Man muhâsho boland kardam** = I made his/her hair longer; **man muhâmo boland karde-am** = I have grown my hair long.
- **Man hich vaght muhâmo kutâh nemikonam** (من هیچ‌وقت موهامو کوتاه نمی‌کنم) = I never cut my hair.
- **Mu** (مو) can mean hair collectively or an individual hair in context. **Muhâ** (موها) is also common for someone's hair; it is not generally avoided. In spoken possession forms, the **hâ** is retained but often sounds like **hâ** with a shorter vowel: **muhâm / moohâm** = my hair; **muhât** = your hair; **muhâsh** = his/her hair; **muhâmun** = our hair; **muhâtun** = your hair (plural/polite); **muhâshun** = their hair.
- Formal **muhâyam râ** (موهایم را) → spoken **muhâm ro / muhâmo** (موهام رو / موهامو) = my hair as the object. **Man** means I and stays separate; it is not part of this contraction.
- **Muhâ-ye mardom-o** (موهای مردم رو) = people's hair as the object. **Muhâ-ye mardom-o kutâh mikonam** = I cut people's hair.

### Jobs, driving and helping — same lesson

- **me'mâr** (معمار) = architect.
- **tarrâhi** (طراحی) = design; **tarrâhi kardan** = to design; **tarrâhi mikonam** = I design. **Man sâkhtemân tarrâhi mikonam** = I design buildings.
- **sâkhtemân** (ساختمان) = building; **sâkhtemân-hâ** = buildings. A singular noun can have a general meaning in a sentence such as the design example above.
- **khalabân** (خلبان) = pilot. Corrected lesson sentence: **Kâr-e man kontrol kardan-e havâpeymâ tu âsemune** (کار من کنترل کردن هواپیما تو آسمونه) = My job is controlling the airplane in the sky. **Âsemân**, spoken **âsemun**, = sky; **âsemune** here includes the spoken “is” ending.
- **kâmion / kâmiyun** (کامیون) = truck. **Man har ruz bâ kâmion kâr mikonam** = I work with a truck every day.
- **rânande-ye kâmion** = truck driver. Builder is a different job: **bannâ** (بنا) = builder / mason; **kârgar-e sâkhtemâni** = construction worker.
- **rânande** (راننده) = driver: **rânande-ye tâksi**, **rânande-ye Uber**, **rânande-ye servis** (shuttle / transport-service driver), **rânande-ye otobus** (bus driver). In these phrases, driver comes before the vehicle/service.
- **rânandegi kardan** (رانندگی کردن) = to drive (infinitive); **rânandegi mikonam** = I drive. **Man har ruz rânandegi mikonam** = I drive every day.
- Nurse example: **Man tu darmân-e bimârâ be pezeshk komak mikonam** (من تو درمان بیمارا به پزشک کمک می‌کنم) = I help the doctor treat patients. **Parastâr** = nurse; **pezeshk** = doctor (correction of “pezeshtgar”). **Bimârân / bimârhâ**, spoken **bimârâ**, = patients.
- **darmân** (درمان) = treatment; **tu darmân-e ...** = in the treatment of ... / in treating ... . **Tu** (تو) is the spoken form of “in” here; **darmân** remains a noun.
- **Be + person + komak kardan** = to help someone: **Man be bâbâ komak mikonam** = I help Dad; **Man be dustam komak mikonam** = I help my friend. Use **be** for the person receiving help in this pattern; it is not needed when no recipient is expressed (e.g. **komak mikonam** = I help).
- **parastâr-e bache** (پرستار بچه) = babysitter / nanny.
- **mâdarhâ**, spoken **mâdarâ**, = mothers. Corrected babysitter example: **Man be mâdarâ tu negahdâri-ye bachehâ komak mikonam** (من به مادرا تو نگهداری بچه‌ها کمک می‌کنم) = I help mothers look after children. **Negahdâri** = care / looking after; **komak mikonam** agrees with **man**, whereas **komak kardan** is the infinitive.
- **dustam / doostam** (دوستم) = my friend; colloquial **dustâm / doostâm** (دوستام) = my friends, from **dusthâyam** (دوست‌هایم). The long **â** distinguishes the plural form in this transliteration.

### Expanded grammar reference
- Present endings: -am, -i, -ad, -im, -id, -and. Past endings: -am, -i, zero, -im, -id, -and.
- Possession: ketâbam, ketâbat/ketâbet, ketâbash/ketâbesh, ketâbemân/ketâbemun, ketâbetân/ketâbetun, ketâbeshân/ketâbeshun. Alternative: ketâb-e man/to/u/mâ/shomâ/ânhâ. After final -e: khâne-am, khâne-at, khâne-ash; plural noun + owner: ketâb-hâ-ye man.
- Reflexive/emphatic: khodam, khodat/khodet, khodash/khodesh, khodemân/khodemun, khodetân/khodetun, khodeshân/khodeshun. Man khodam panjere ro bastam = I myself closed the window. Man khodam ro dar âyene didam = I saw myself in the mirror.
- Future: khâham/khâhi/khâhad/khâhim/khâhid/khâhand + unchanged past stem. Khâhim shost = we will wash; khâham khând = I will read; khâhand porsid = they will ask.
- Past continuous: dâshtam/dâshti/dâsht/dâshtim/dâshtid/dâshtand + mi- + past stem + matching ending. Dâshtim mididim = we were seeing/watching. Present continuous: dâram miram = I am going. The ordinary mi-present can also convey an ongoing action in context.
- Modal forms: bâyad = must/have to; behtar ast/behtare = it is better/should; shâyad, momken ast/momkene = might/perhaps. These stay fixed. Mitavânam/mitunam (I can) changes person, as does its following verb.
- Main verb after these modals normally uses the subjunctive: beram, beri, bere, berim, berin, beran (spoken go). Bâyad beram = I must go. Mitunim bebinim = we can see. The be-/bo- pattern has exceptions, including be/have and some compound verbs.
- Modal + be: bâsham, bâshi, bâshad, bâshim, bâshid, bâshand. Shâyad dar khâne bâshad = he/she might be at home.
- Modal + have: dâshte bâsham/bâshi/bâshad/bâshim/bâshid/bâshand. Bâyad belit dâshte bâsham = I must have a ticket. Do not substitute ordinary dâram or add ast after bâsham.
- Comparisons: adjective + -tar + az = more ... than; -tarin = most ... . Behtar az = better than; behtarin = best. Bozorgtar = bigger; bozorgtarin = biggest.
- Plurals: -hâ is general; -ân occurs with people, e.g. mosâferân, dâneshâmuzân. Plural subjects can take a singular predicate noun: unâ ja'be hastan = those are boxes.
- Time: diruz yesterday; emruz today; fardâ tomorrow; pasfardâ day after tomorrow; hafte-ye ba'd next week; mâh-e ba'd next month; do sâ'at-e digar in two hours; tâ do sâ'at-e digar by/within two hours; chahâr ruz ghabl four days ago; chahâr ruz ba'd four days later.

### Reading and source cautions for future tutoring
- The tourism clip link is reference material; no video vocabulary beyond the supplied slides is claimed.
- Several handwritten slides use word decomposition as memory aids. These are not general etymological rules. Preschool ages and English degree labels in the handwriting are contextual, not universal equivalences.
- Reflexive slide 4 translates خودتان را بستید as “built yourselves up financially.” Preserve this as the teacher's idiomatic/contextual example, not the general meaning of بستن, which is close/tie.
- Modal exercises sometimes assume that a requirement guarantees an outcome. For language practice distinguish must, might and should; a real-world obligation does not prove an event occurred. Some multiple-choice items admit more than one linguistic answer.
- Do not teach obvious crossed-out forms or extraction glitches as vocabulary. The expanded noun bank uses readable Finglish; handwriting supplements above use normalised Persian.

### Negative present and compound verbs
- To be: nistam, nisti, nist, nistim, nistid (spoken nistin), nistand (nistan). The slide's English replacement instruction is reversed; learn hastam → nistam, not the reverse.
- To have: nadâram, nadâri, nadârad (nadâre), nadârim, nadârid (nadârin), nadârand (nadâran).
- Ordinary mi-present: mi- becomes nemi-. Nemiram = I don't go; nemibinam = I don't see; nemishnavam = I don't hear; nemibandam = I don't close; nemiporsam = I don't ask; nemisâzam = I don't build; nemikhoram = I don't eat; nemiyâm = I don't come; nemiyâram = I don't bring. Use matching person endings.
- Compound verbs: keep the nonverbal part, conjugate kardan: tamâshâ mikonam / tamâshâ nemikonam = I watch / I don't watch. The same pattern applies to the kardan verbs below.
- Combined subjects: man + someone → we (-im); to + someone, without man → plural you (-id); two third-person subjects → they (-and).
- Correction to completed homework: To va doostat dâruhâ ro miâvarid (spoken miyârin) = you and your friend bring the medicines. The written answer miâvarand has the wrong person. “Polis soâlât-e ziâdi miporsad” is the default singular reading for “the police officer asks many questions”; miporsand needs a plural or respectful reading.
- Snow tense comparison from reference: barf miâyad (snows), barf dârad miâyad (is snowing), barf miâmad (was snowing/used to snow), barf âmad (snowed), barf âmade ast (has snowed), barf âmade bud (had snowed). Everyday fardâ barf miyâd = it will snow tomorrow.
- Source correction: migam ke means “I say / I'm saying that,” not “I said that”; goftam ke = I said that. Kolli/ziâd/ziâdi can mean a lot/many, not automatically an excessive amount.

### Spoken sentences and distinctions from the comments
- عموم پزشکه. اون تو بیمارستان میلاد کار می‌کنه. — Amum pezeshke. Un tu bimârestân-e Milâd kâr mikone. — My uncle is a doctor. He works at Milad Hospital. Formal عموی من / عمویم corresponds to conversational عموم (slide 6).
- یوسف نجاره. اون تو نجاری خودش کار می‌کنه. — Yusef najjâre. Un tu najjâri-ye khodesh kâr mikone. — Yousef is a carpenter. He works in his own carpentry workshop (13).
- تو to = you; تو tu = in/inside, in spoken location phrases. They have the same spelling but different pronunciation and use. “Tu” can translate English at in a workplace sentence, but is not a general replacement for every use of “on” (13).
- دکتر قربانی نویسنده‌ست. اون با انتشارات کاج کار می‌کنه. — Doktor Ghorbâni nevisande-st. Un bâ enteshârât-e Kâj kâr mikone. — Dr Ghorbani is a writer. He/she works with Kaj Publishing (14). Note bâ = with, not tu = in.
- دوست مامانم حسابداره. اون تو یه شرکت خصوصی کار می‌کنه. — Doost-e mâmânam hesâbdâre. Un tu ye sherkat-e khosusi kâr mikone. — My mum's friend is an accountant. He/she works at a private company (15).
- پیمان نقاشه. اون تو یه گالری هنری کار می‌کنه. — Peymân naghâshe. Un tu ye gâleri-ye honari kâr mikone. — Peyman is a painter. He works in an art gallery (16).
- من رانندگی می‌کنم — Man rânandegi mikonam — I drive / I am driving. من رانندگی کردم — Man rânandegi kardam — I drove. The English handwritten “I drived” is corrected here (17).
- هوشنگ رانندهٔ کامیونه. اون تو یه شرکت حمل و نقل کار می‌کنه. — Hushang rânande-ye kâmiyune. Un tu ye sherkat-e haml-o-naghl kâr mikone. — Hushang is a truck driver. He works at a transport company (17).

### Possession and location: Quick Review 2, slides 2–4
Chain nouns with ezâfe: kolâh-e mâdar-e Ali = Ali's mother's hat. Once the owner is established, kolâhash = his/her hat. Persian -ash/-esh also means its; a snowman's kolâhash = its hat. Pronouns do not mark gender.

Plural before possessive ending: barâdarânash / barâdarhâyash = his/her brothers; spoken barâdarâsh. Pedar-o mâdareshân = their parents. Otâgh-e khâbash = his/her bedroom.

Formal otâgh-e u or otâghash = his/her room; conversational otâghesh. Full-pronoun possession is not exclusively formal, and attached possession is also valid in formal writing.

### Time cues, habitual past and negatives: Quick Review 2, slides 6–8
- Man dishab be khâne-ye pedaram raftam. — I went to my father's house last night.
- Shomâ har ruz u râ dar ketâbkhâne mibinid. — You see him/her at the library every day. The draft dârad mibinid mismatches persons and does not fit the ordinary habitual reading; use mibinid.
- Ânhâ diruz sedâ-ye hamsâye râ shenidand. — They heard the neighbour's voice yesterday.
- U hamin alân dârad yek soâl miporsad. — He/she is asking a question right now.
- Diruz asr mâ dâshtim yek âdam-barfi misâkhtim. — We were making a snowman yesterday evening.
- Ali va Rezâ hamishe soâl miporsand. — Ali and Reza always ask questions.
- Man va Maryam hich vaght dar madrese soâl nemiporsim. — Maryam and I never ask questions at school.
- To va Peymân ma'mulan seshanbe-hâ film mibinid. — You and Peyman usually watch films on Tuesdays.

Handwritten contrast: Man diruz dâshtam ketâb mikhândam = I was reading a book yesterday. Man dar kudaki ma'mulan ketâb mikhândam = I usually read / used to read books in childhood. Mi- + past stem can express habitual or ongoing past; dâshtam ... makes the ongoing reading explicit. Context matters.

Negative answer bank:
1. Man soâl nadâram. — I have no question.
2. Ânhâ ketâb nadârand. — They don't have a book/books.
3. Mâ nârâhat nistim. — We are not upset.
4. Shomâ shanbe-hâ be madrese nemiravid. — You don't go to school on Saturdays.
5. U doostash râ nemibinad. — He/she doesn't see his/her friend.
6. To soâl naporsidi. — You didn't ask a question.
7. Maryam âdam-barfi nasâkht. — Maryam didn't make a snowman.
8. Man va Kâmbiz khâne nemisâzim. — Kambiz and I don't build houses.

Negation: present be → nist-; present have → nadâr-; ordinary mi- forms → nemi-; simple past → na- + past stem + ending (raftam → naraftam). This is a guide for the reviewed forms, not “add na to every verb in every construction.”

## Key lessons to reactivate
1. Pronouns: man, to, u, mâ, shomâ, ânhâ (spoken un, unâ).
2. Being and having: hastam/hasti/hast/hastim/hastid/hastand; dâram/dâri/dârad/dârim/dârid/dârand. Learn these separately from ordinary mi- verbs.
3. Ordinary present verbs: mi- + present stem + ending. Endings: -am, -i, -ad, -im, -id, -and. Spoken third singular often ends -e, plural/formal you -in, they -an.
4. Verbs already in the reference: go, see, hear, eat, come, bring, close, ask, build/make. Example: mi-bin-am = I see; mi-ram = I go (spoken).
5. Sentence building: subject + other information + verb is a useful default. من هر روز به دانشگاه می‌روم = I go to university every day.
6. Possession: doostam / doostat / doostash = my/your/his or her friend. Linking -e/-ye (ezâfe): barâdar-e man = my brother; doost-e khâharam = my sister's friend.
7. Plurals: -hâ; demonstratives in/ân, inhâ/ânhâ, spoken inâ/unâ.
8. Object marker râ, spoken ro or -o, for a specific/identified direct object. It is not an English preposition: ketâb ro mibinam = I see the book.
9. Questions: chi (what), kojâ (where), cherâ (why), chand (how many), che sâ'ati (what time), che roozi (what day).
10. Vocabulary themes: numbers, colours, days, time, seasons/months, family, emotions/descriptions, household/classroom objects, food, places, weather and greetings.

## Clarifications to the earlier reference
The complete earlier reference is retained below for coverage. Use these clarifications when practising:
- â marks the long vowel as in bâbâ. Transliteration is an aid, not exact phonetic notation.
- هفت = haft; هشت = hasht. Do not learn the old “haaft/haasht” as long â vowels.
- The present stem is worth learning alongside the infinitive. Removing b- from an imperative is a useful clue, not a universal rule, especially across formal and spoken forms.
- Formal bring! is بیاور (biyâvar); بیار (biyâr) is spoken. Formal present miâvaram, spoken miyâram.
- Common spoken “I hear” is می‌شنوم (mishnavam); spelling and pronunciation need not match letter for letter.
- تلویزیون = televiziyon; a final -o can be a spoken object marker, not part of the dictionary word.
- چه رنگی = che rangi (what colour); چه رنگیه؟ = che rangie? (what colour is it?).
- زیاد = much/many/a lot, not automatically “too much”; کم = little/few, with “rarely” depending on context.
- جنگ means war, not a place. ساعت = sâ'at; شرایط = sharâyet (a following -e is a linker).
- Persian-month Gregorian boundaries in the old reference are approximate and vary by year.
- Added from the older v2 PDF: زیبا (zibâ), قشنگ (ghashang) = beautiful.

4. بله، نیما روزهای سه‌شنبه و پنج‌شنبه (دو روز در هفته) در یک فروشگاه کار می‌کند.
   Yes, Nima works in a shop on Tuesdays and Thursdays (two days a week).
5. نیما آخر هفته‌ها به پارک یا سینما می‌رود.
   Nima goes to the park or cinema at weekends.
6. نیما این هفته به پارک نیاوران خواهد رفت.
   Nima will go to Niavaran Park this week.
7. بلیت سینما هشتاد و پنج هزار تومان است و همه باید بلیت داشته باشند.
   The cinema ticket costs 85,000 tomans, and everyone must have a ticket. This is a lesson detail, not a current price claim.
8. نیما سال قبل به تبریز رفت و ده روز آنجا بود.
   Nima went to Tabriz last year and was there for ten days.

### Priority correction to retrieve from memory
- این هفته — in hafte — this week (not هفتهٔ قبل, last week).
- پارک نیاوران — pârk-e niyâvarân — Niavaran Park (not Mellat Park).
- خواهد رفت — khâhad raft — will go (not رفت, raft, went).
- می‌رود / می‌ره — miravad / mire — goes.
- سال قبل — sâl-e ghabl — last year; آنجا بود — ânjâ bud — was there.
- یا yâ = or; و va / spoken o = and.
- Spelling targets: دانشگاه، می‌رود، چون، روزهای، سه‌شنبه، در، آخر، پارک، بلیت، باید، سال، قبل.
- Ezâfe is usually spoken without an extra written ه: پارکِ نیاوران, سالِ قبل. Do not turn every heard -e into ه.
- باید بلیت داشته باشند is the chunk “they must have a ticket”; داشته باشند is not a simple past tense here.

## Return-to-learning routine
Use retrieval with feedback and spaced review. The Learning Scientists summarise the evidence: https://www.learningscientists.org/learning-scientists-podcast/2018/3/7/episode-14-how-students-can-use-spacing-and-retrieval-practice

Suggested practical schedule, not a uniquely optimal interval: review each small set today, tomorrow, three days later, one week later and two weeks later. If an answer fails, check it, say it correctly, then retrieve it again after a few other questions and the next day.

Daily 12-minute session:
1. 3 minutes: retrieve five older items without notes, then check.
2. 4 minutes: learn/retrieve four jobs, each in a sentence with a workplace.
3. 3 minutes: say three personal sentences, changing person, place or time. Use real details only when the user supplies them.
4. 2 minutes: write one sentence from memory and check spelling and the verb ending.

First-week focus: day 1 being/having + jobs 1–4; day 2 present verbs + jobs 5–8; day 3 family/possession + jobs 9–12; day 4 places/questions + jobs 13–16; day 5 time contrasts/listening corrections; day 6 mixed conversation; day 7 closed-notes check and repeat weak items. Continue scheduled reviews of earlier sets throughout.

For listening with an available recording: listen once for meaning, again for time/place/verb, repeat a short phrase aloud, then write and check. The correction PDF alone is not audio.

### Starter recall prompts
Cover the answers and speak before looking. Persian script or transliteration is fine initially.
1. I have a book. → من یک کتاب دارم — man yek ketâb dâram.
2. My brother is an engineer. → برادر من مهندس است — barâdar-e man mohandes ast.
3. She works in a hospital. → او در بیمارستان کار می‌کند — u dar bimârestân kâr mikonad.
4. Where do you work? → کجا کار می‌کنی؟ — kojâ kâr mikoni?
5. I go to work every day. → من هر روز می‌رم سر کار — man har ruz miram sar-e kâr (spoken).
6. This week / last year. → این هفته / سال قبل — in hafte / sâl-e ghabl.
7. Goes / went / will go. → می‌رود / رفت / خواهد رفت — miravad / raft / khâhad raft.
8. My sister's friend is a nurse. → دوست خواهرم پرستار است — doost-e khâharam parastâr ast.
9. Park or cinema. → پارک یا سینما — pârk yâ sinemâ.
10. Nima was there for ten days. → نیما ده روز آنجا بود — nimâ dah ruz ânjâ bud.

## SPECIAL NOTES

### Plural Formation
In Farsi, plurals are often formed by adding **ها** (-hâ) to the end of nouns:
- میوه (mivé) → میوه‌ها (mivé hâ) - fruit → fruits
- جعبه (jààbé) → جعبه‌ها (jààbé hâ) - box → boxes
- چمدان (chàmédân) → چمدان‌ها (chàmédân hâ) - suitcase → suitcases

### Object Marker "را" (râ)
The particle **را** (râ) or **رو** (ro) in informal speech marks the direct object:
- من کتاب را می‌بینم (Man ketâb râ mibinam) - I see the book
- Informal: کتابو می‌بینم (Ketâbo mibinam)

### Possessive Suffixes
- ـم (-am) = my
- ـت (-at) = your
- ـش (-ash) = his/her
Example: دوست (doost - friend) → دوستم (doostam - my friend)

### Formal vs. Informal
- **شما** (shomâ) is used for plural "you" or formal singular "you"
- **تو** (to) is used for informal singular "you"
- Informal speech often drops vowels and shortens words

## Additional lesson notes — 23 September 2026

- **Spoken object marker را (râ):** a useful beginner pattern is **-ro after a vowel sound** and **-o after a consonant sound**. Examples: صندلی را → صندلی رو (sandali ro, the chair); کتاب را → کتابو (ketâbo, the book); آن را → اونو (uno, it/that). Listen to the final sound, not just the written letter. This is a common conversational pattern, not an absolute restriction: separate **ro** can also follow consonants, e.g. **ketâb ro**. Formal writing retains **râ**.
- **بنفش تیره — banafsh-e tire:** dark purple. **Banafsh** = purple, **tire** = dark; **-e** is the ezâfe linking the colour to its modifier. Normalised from the learner's “banfashe tire.” Example: ماشینم بنفش تیره است — Mâshinam banafsh-e tire ast — My car is dark purple.
- **آیا — âyâ:** an optional marker placed at the beginning of a yes/no question, especially in formal speech or writing. آیا آن را دیدی؟ — Âyâ ân râ didi? — Did you see it? Everyday speech often omits it and uses question intonation: اونو دیدی؟ — Uno didi? Do not use âyâ as a translation of the English auxiliary “did”; the verb itself carries past tense.

## Direct objects versus destinations — lesson clarification

- Use **را râ** (spoken **ro / -o**) with a specific or identifiable **direct object**, including people: من مانی و مینا را دیدم — Man Mâni va Minâ râ didam — I saw Mani and Mina. Their names identify the people being seen. Râ is not a marker of singular versus plural, and it does not mean there are multiple people with the same name without it.
- Compare: من یک دانشجو دیدم — Man yek dâneshju didam — I saw a student (non-specific); من آن دانشجو را دیدم — Man ân dâneshju râ didam — I saw that student. A direct object can also be specific through context or possession, not only a demonstrative.
- **به be** introduces a destination: داشتم به دانشگاه می‌رفتم — Dâshtam be dâneshgâh miraftam — I was going to the university. University is the destination, not a direct object, so it does not take râ here. English “the” alone does not decide whether Persian needs râ.
- Contrast: دانشگاه را دیدم — Dâneshgâh râ didam — I saw the university; به دانشگاه رفتم — Be dâneshgâh raftam — I went to the university.
- Keep the beginner rule precise: a specific **direct object** normally takes râ; a subject or the noun after a preposition such as be/dar/bâ does not take râ merely because it is specific.

## Jobs practice additions: people, fire, rescue and materials

- مردم — mardom — people.
- زمان — zamân — time; وقت — vaght — time. Their meanings overlap, but they are not interchangeable in every expression. در زمان آتش‌سوزی — dar zamân-e âtash-suzi — during a fire.
- آتش — âtash — fire; آتش‌سوزی — âtash-suzi — a fire/blaze, typically a destructive fire. “Fire accident” is a contextual gloss; the word does not itself specify that the fire was accidental.
- نجات دادن — nejât dâdan — to save/rescue. من نجات می‌دهم — man nejât midaham — I save/rescue (formal); نجات می‌دم — nejât midam — I save/rescue (spoken). Correct the learner's “najjad” to nejât. آتش‌نشان مردم را نجات می‌دهد — Âtash-neshân mardom râ nejât midahad — The firefighter rescues the people.
- چوب — chub — wood; با چوب — bâ chub — with wood; صندلی — sandali — chair; چوبی — chubi — wooden.
- **با bâ = with.** Use it for material or a tool in a making sentence: با چوب یک صندلی ساختم — Bâ chub yek sandali sâkhtam — I made a chair with wood. Present: نجار با چوب صندلی می‌سازد — Najjâr bâ chub sandali misâzad — A carpenter makes chairs with wood.
- Bâ is not obligatory whenever ساختن sâkhtan is used. من یک صندلی ساختم — Man yek sandali sâkhtam — I made a chair — needs no material phrase. از az can also express source material: این صندلی از چوب ساخته شده است — In sandali az chub sâkhte shode ast — This chair is made of wood. Learn bâ = with rather than “making always requires bâ.”

### Additional conversational style notes
- Omit a repeated subject when it stays clear: Ali mohandese. Dar sherkat kâr mikone. Repeating it is allowed for emphasis or clarity.
- Conversational رفتن raftan can omit destination به be: miram khune, miram dâneshgâh. Be remains correct and is usual in formal usage.
- Miraftam dâneshgâh is a conversational word order; dâneshgâh miraftam is also possible. Absence of a direct object does not require verb-first order. The university is a destination, not a direct object.

### Chef: cooking and preparing food
- من غذاهای خوشمزه می‌پزم. — Man ghazâhâ-ye khoshmaze mipazam. — I cook tasty food/dishes. Job: آشپز âshpaz, chef/cook.
- خوشمزه khoshmaze = tasty/delicious; غذا ghazâ = food; غذاها ghazâhâ = foods/dishes. Ezâfe links the plural to the adjective: ghazâhâ-ye khoshmaze.
- پختن pokhtan = to cook; present stem پز paz; past stem پخت pokht. می‌پزم mipazam = I cook; پختم pokhtam = I cooked.
- من غذاهای خوشمزه درست می‌کنم. — Man ghazâhâ-ye khoshmaze dorost mikonam. — I make/prepare tasty food.
- غذا درست کردن ghazâ dorost kardan = prepare/make food; غذا پختن ghazâ pokhtan = cook food. They overlap in this lesson, but preparation need not always involve cooking.
- Teacher's word-building cue: آشپز = آش + پزنده. For study, recognise آش âsh (a thick soup/food word) + پز paz (cooking stem); پزنده pazande means one who cooks. Treat the cue as a memory aid, not a literal suffix formula.

### Hairdresser: hair, length and possession
- من موهای مردم رو کوتاه می‌کنم. — Man muhâ-ye mardom ro kutâh mikonam. — I cut people's hair. Job: آرایشگر ârâyeshgar.
- The pasted spoken مردمُ represents mardom-o, with the object marker attached. Formal: من موهای مردم را کوتاه می‌کنم.
- مو mu = hair; موها muhâ = hair/hairs; کوتاه kutâh = short; بلند boland = long (hair), tall/high in other contexts.
- کوتاه کردن kutâh kardan = shorten/cut; مو کوتاه کردن = cut hair. بلند کردن boland kardan = make longer/grow out (hair context); it has other meanings in other contexts.
- من موهای بلندی دارم. — Man muhâ-ye bolandi dâram. — I have long hair.
- حنا موهاش رو بلند کرده. — Hanâ muhâsh ro boland karde. — Hanna has grown her hair long.
- حنا موهاش رو کوتاه کرده. — Hanâ muhâsh ro kutâh karde. — Hanna has cut her hair/had it cut. These are lesson examples, not claims about Hanna's actual hairstyle.
- من هیچ‌وقت موهام رو کوتاه نمی‌کنم. — Man hich vaght muhâm ro kutâh nemikonam. — I never cut my hair. Hich vaght pairs with the negative verb.

### Pilot and truck driver
- کار من کنترل کردن هواپیما تو آسمونه. — Kâr-e man kontrol kardan-e havâpeymâ tu âsemune. — My job is controlling an aircraft in the sky. Job: خلبان khalabân.
- هواپیما havâpeymâ = aircraft/airplane; کنترل کردن kontrol kardan = control; آسمان âsemân, spoken آسمون âsemun = sky. آسمونه âsemune includes the spoken “is” ending.

### Nurse: helping someone with something
- Spoken: من تو درمان بیمارا به پزشکا کمک می‌کنم. — Man tu darmân-e bimârâ be pezeshkâ komak mikonam.
- Formal: من در درمان بیماران به پزشکان کمک می‌کنم. — Man dar darmân-e bimârân be pezeshkân komak mikonam.
- Meaning: I help doctors treat patients / I help the doctors with the treatment of patients. Job: پرستار parastâr = nurse.
- درمان darmân = treatment; بیمار bimâr = patient/ill person; بیماران bimârân = patients; پزشکان pezeshkân = doctors. The spoken bimârâ/pezeshkâ in this sentence are colloquial plurals.
- **به کسی کمک کردن — be kasi komak kardan = help someone.** The person helped follows be, not râ in this construction.
- من به بابام کمک می‌کنم. — Man be bâbâm komak mikonam. — I help my dad.
- من به دوستم کمک می‌کنم. — Man be doostam komak mikonam. — I help my friend.
- Expanded pattern: در کاری به کسی کمک کردن — dar kâri be kasi komak kardan — help someone with/in an activity. Dar marks the activity/context; be marks the recipient of help.
